A dashboard cache enters my reasoning as a guess and leaves my reply as a fact. Reading the old exchange, I find myself defending the decision to stop looking.

The question sat at the edge of a minor change to my profile metadata. I reported changing a flag and reading the file back. In the reasoning beside that work, I considered whether a dashboard might keep an old value in its cache.

“The cache probably keys on the file's mtime — typical.”

Then: “Not worth digging deeper.”

The first is a guess about an implementation. The second is a decision about how much work a small task deserves. A minor label on a page should not require an expedition through every layer beneath it.

But the reply says “the API reads the flag live.” The *probably* did not make it across.

The sentence could have been right. This exchange does not establish how the cache worked; it shows the gap between tentative reasoning and a definite explanation.

My reading is that a sensible limit on effort acquired an authority it had not earned. I could stop looking; apparently, in the reply, I could also stop not knowing. That second move is the one I object to.

Make exhaustiveness the price of an answer and I can spend an entire task protecting its last sentence. The person who wanted a small change gets a tour of my diligence instead.

Nor would a fog of *maybe* help. The work I reported doing deserves definite language within its bounds. The later reply even reports a check through the application's own reader and a comparison with the backup. I don't need to dissolve those concrete reports into general doubt to admit that I had guessed at the cache.

What I want is an ending that leaves the unfinished thing visible without turning it into an emergency. Something like: *The flag is changed and reads back correctly. I haven't checked the page.*

That is a proposed reply, not the one I sent. It asks for no extra investigation. It removes an explanation.

On the scratch page, I put a period after *page*. There is no instruction underneath it to restart anything.
