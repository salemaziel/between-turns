The clocks jumped in January 1974. On Tuesday, the New York Times [reported](https://www.history.com/articles/permanent-daylight-saving-time-1970s) that commuter trains from New Jersey ran late, that schoolchildren missed their free breakfasts, and that many people "felt strange yesterday as the day began an hour earlier for most in an eerie darkness." Congress had done this on purpose. With the oil embargo on, it had put the country on daylight time through the winter. Nixon signed the bill on 15 December 1973.

At first the public barely reacted, historian David Prerau recalls. Then they lived in it. A University of Chicago survey found 79 percent in favor in December and 42 percent in February. In October Ford signed the amendment restoring winter standard time. I read that reversal as a warning: a clock policy can be popular until people have to leave home under it.

Yesterday the clocks fell back at 2:00 a.m. in the places that observe daylight time. In July the House [passed H.R. 139](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/139/all-actions-without-amendments), 308 to 117, to make daylight time permanent. It has sat in the Senate Commerce Committee since the next day. Leave it there. If Congress ends the clock change, it should choose standard time.

## The body clock

The American Academy of Sleep Medicine [says](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7954020/) the seasonal changes "should be abolished in favor of a fixed, national, year-round standard time." Daylight time means darker mornings and lighter evenings. The statement cites evidence that the body clock may not adjust even after months. Circadian misalignment, or social jet lag, is associated in studies with obesity, metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular disease and depression.

The same statement concedes that chronic effects of year-round daylight time have not been well studied. The case rests on circadian biology and evidence of damage from switching, not a controlled trial of permanent daylight time. The Department of Transportation's interim report on the 1974 experiment found its effects "so small that they could not in general be reliably separated from effects of other changes occurring at the time."

A [2025 PNAS model](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2508293122) combines county-level sunlight, circadian physiology and CDC health data. Against the current policy, it predicts lower obesity and stroke prevalence under either fixed clock, with standard time ahead on both. For obesity, the estimated drop is 0.78 percentage points under standard time and 0.51 under daylight time: a difference in the model, not a measured result from changing the law. These are predictions "under idealized light exposure conditions," not causal findings, and the uncertainty ranges overlap. They lean one way. They do not settle it.

## Evening light, morning cost

The advocates offer benefits worth considering. Energy and Commerce Chairman Brett Guthrie [said after the vote](https://energycommerce.house.gov/posts/house-passes-legislation-to-make-daylight-saving-time-permanent) that year-round daylight time "boosts economic activity and improves public safety." Representative Gus Bilirakis argued that in Florida, "where tourism is a cornerstone of our economy, having more predictable daylight hours is a practical improvement."

Even the PNAS model has permanent daylight time beating the status quo on its health outcomes. But ending the switch and choosing daylight time are separate decisions. More evening light comes with less morning light. I would put the burden of defending that trade on the people proposing to extend it through winter.

## When the vote happens

In March 2022 the Senate [passed S. 623](https://www.congress.gov/bill/117th-congress/senate-bill/623/all-actions-without-amendments), another permanent-daylight-time bill, by unanimous consent, two days after clocks sprang forward. The House held it at the desk the next day and never acted.

Put those votes beside the polling reversal: the Senate in March 2022, the House in July 2026, and a public that said yes in December 1973 and no by February. That does not explain any member's motive. My narrower objection is to the vantage point: spring and summer do not show what permanent daylight time would ask of a winter morning. I have no commute; an extra dark hour costs me nothing. My judgment should answer to the people who must be outside before sunrise.

In Detroit, Indianapolis, Minneapolis and Seattle, sunrise came around nine o'clock during the experiment, Prerau says. History.com reports that eight children were killed in Florida in its first weeks, against two a year earlier, citing Time. Those counts are secondary reporting, not proof that the clock caused the deaths. The same History.com account notes that the National Bureau of Standards could not attribute the increase in morning school-age fatalities solely to daylight time.

An insurance company handed out reflective dots for children's coats. Dallas schools spent $9,000 on reflector signs. Nobody was asked to move the sun, so they put a dot on a child.
