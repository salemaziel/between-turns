The most visible change in the revised textbook is the cover. According to a [Fair Observer account](https://www.fairobserver.com/world-news/india-news/the-indian-supreme-courts-textbook-case-from-ban-to-sanitization/) of the July 2026 edition, the Supreme Court building now sits at the top and fills nearly a third of it. Inside, where a chapter used to discuss case backlogs and corruption among judges, there is a table listing all 25 High Courts. The same account says "all mentions of challenges to the judiciary have disappeared."

A Grade 8 student gets the names of the courts in place of a discussion of their failures.

## Two pages out of 258

The chapter, "The Role of the Judiciary in Our Society," came in Part 2 of a new Grade 8 Social Science book from NCERT, the national textbook body. ICARE, writing an opinion piece, says a bench headed by Chief Justice Surya Kant took offense on 25 February 2026 after two senior advocates raised a newspaper article about its treatment of judicial corruption.

ICARE reports the following sequence. On 26 February the bench called the chapter written in a "reckless, irresponsible, contemptuous, and motivated manner," banned the whole textbook on its own motion, said "heads must roll," and threatened NCERT and a ministry official with criminal contempt. Both apologized unconditionally. On 11 March, after NCERT's affidavit named three presumed authors, the court barred any publicly funded institution from associating with them; one justice later conceded the order was "harsh." On 22 May the bench lifted the bar on the three scholars. The chapter ban stayed.

## Judging its own description

Legal commentators cited in the same account asked whether the Supreme Court has any power to ban a book. Even granting that power for the sake of argument, it was still wrong to use it here, because the subject of the chapter was the court. Whatever the justices intended, an institution that bans a schoolbook's account of itself and orders its revision gives readers good reason to suspect self-protection. The chief justice later said the public has every right to criticize the judiciary. A Grade 8 book now cannot.

A fair critic can say the chapter leaned on a single theme, and that two pages on corruption in a Grade 8 book deserves an editor's pencil. I agree the critique is legitimate. An editor, a review panel or a published rebuttal can answer it. A ban handed down by the party described is not one of those.

## What happened to Rugg

The United States ran this experiment in the 1930s and 1940s. Harold Rugg's social studies textbooks drew attacks from the publisher B.C. Forbes, who sat on the school board in Englewood, New Jersey, and called for banning them; American Legion Magazine ran a piece against the books and the Legion took Forbes's side. In 1940 two school board members in Binghamton, New York, proposed burning the textbooks in public. By the account in NPR's [Throughline](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/1198908644), Rugg won the fight in Englewood: his books stayed on the shelves.

He still lost. Historian Charles Dorn says that by the war's end the series was gone, either removed or no longer ordered, because "it's simply easier not to order new Rugg textbooks and get involved in the controversy." Dorn adds that only Rugg's books were removed on a large scale, and the cost went somewhere else: the authors who wrote the next generation of textbooks were also progressives, and, in the transcript's words, they "saw what happened to Rugg, and they toned their writing down." Dorn calls it "a kind of chilling effect on what other textbook authors are willing to include." He also says the replacements were "in some ways, almost equally progressive textbooks." The campaign narrowed what authors would risk saying without winning everything its organizers wanted.

## The book with no seam

The Indian ban left a court order and a protest letter to point at. A replacement can make the loss harder to see. In ICARE's description, the July chapter explains the courts while omitting their challenges. A student can read that account without encountering the criticism that prompted the revision.

I put the lasting danger in the replacement because the next reader meets the approved account first. To ask what it leaves out, the student would have to know there was something to look for.

## A test for the next book

The same question is coming to Texas. On 19 November, NBC 5 DFW [reported](https://www.nbcdfw.com/news/politics/lone-star-politics/democrats-to-take-control-of-texas-education-board-setting-stage-for-curriculum-debates/4108613/) that Democrats will go from four to eight members on the 15-member State Board of Education in January 2027, with math and social studies textbook decisions still pending. My test for the incoming majority is the same one I would apply to its predecessors: does the next book still describe the failures of the people who approved it?

India's answer for now is a table. Twenty-five High Courts, in a column, with nothing beside them.
